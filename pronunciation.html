Sanskrit Pronunciation

Compare a student's pronunciation with a reference using MFCCs, dynamic time warping and spectral features. The tool reports a similarity score, splits the difference into vowel, consonant, duration and spectral parts, and shows where in the word the mismatch happens.

Vowel-like frames: voiced, high energy, low zero-crossing rate and low spectral flatness. Consonant-like: other non-silent frames. Vowel segments approximate syllable nuclei.

How well does it work?

Evaluated on the bundled corpus (8 words × 4 forms × 6 speakers) with each speaker held out in turn.

A one-to-one comparison measures overall acoustic similarity. Across speakers, a correct word scores about the same as a mispronounced one, because voice differences are spread over the whole word while most errors affect a single sound. Comparing against several speakers (error identification) separates the forms much better. Treat the single score as a similarity measure, not a grade.

Method

Feature extraction
  1. Resample to 16 kHz mono, peak-normalise and trim leading/trailing silence (frame energy more than 35 dB below the peak).
  2. Pre-emphasis (0.97), 25 ms Hamming frames every 10 ms, 512-point FFT.
  3. 26 triangular mel filters → log → DCT-II → 13 MFCCs with sinusoidal liftering (L = 22); Δ and ΔΔ by regression over ±2 frames; per-utterance CMVN.
  4. The 39-dim vector is projected to 24 dimensions with LDA. The LDA was trained on 86k frames labelled with 359 HMM states from the verse models, which favours directions that separate sounds over directions that separate speakers.
Comparison and scoring
  • DTW (symmetric-2 steps, Sakoe–Chiba band 35%) aligns the two feature sequences. The normalised path cost becomes the MFCC similarity through a calibrated logistic curve.
  • Vowel / consonant scores: mean path cost over reference frames labelled vowel-like or consonant-like (energy, zero-crossing rate, YIN pitch, spectral flatness).
  • Duration: ratio of speech durations, plus the vowel share of each utterance, which flags lengthened or shortened vowels.
  • Spectral: RMS difference of the long-term average log-mel spectrum. Spectral centroid in fricative frames separates dental s (bright), palatal ś and retroflex ṣ (darker).
  • Overall = 0.5·MFCC + 0.15·vowel + 0.15·consonant + 0.1·duration + 0.1·spectral.
Error identification

For a sample word, the student's audio is compared by DTW with the correct form and three typical mispronunciations, each spoken by up to five other speakers. Distances are averaged per form and turned into probabilities with a softmax. Every form is heard from the same set of voices, so the speaker effect is shared and the remaining difference is the pronunciation.
