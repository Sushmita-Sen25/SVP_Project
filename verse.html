HMM-Based Recognition of Bhagavad Gita Shlokas

Play or recite a verse and the system tells you which one it is. Each of the 10 verses has its own left-to-right hidden Markov model over 39-dimensional MFCC features, and the input is scored against all of them with the forward algorithm.

Method

Front end

16 kHz mono → silence trimming → pre-emphasis → 25 ms Hamming frames every 10 ms → 26-band mel filterbank → 13 MFCCs (liftered) → Δ and ΔΔ → per-utterance cepstral mean and variance normalisation. One recitation of about 10 s becomes roughly 1,000 vectors of 39 dimensions.

Model
  • One HMM per verse with N ≈ frames/20 states (34–46). Left-to-right topology: from each state you can stay, move to the next state, or skip one.
  • Emissions are single diagonal-covariance Gaussians, with a variance floor of 5% of the global variance.
  • Training: flat start (uniform segmentation) → 4 Viterbi re-estimation passes → 6 Baum–Welch (EM) passes, all in the log domain.
Recognition

The forward algorithm computes log P(O | λv) for each verse model and the highest score wins. The path must end in the final state, unless partial mode is on, in which case it may end in any state. The relative score is a softmax over the per-frame log-likelihoods. The Viterbi path of the winning model gives the alignment shown above.

Data

Recitations are synthesised with six neural TTS voices (Hindi, Marathi and Nepali). Training uses 4 voices × 3 tempo/pitch settings = 12 recitations per verse. The test clips use the two Nepali voices, which are never seen in training, and the four training voices at tempos not used in training. The full pipeline can be re-run with scripts/generate_audio.py and scripts/train_gita_hmm.mjs.
